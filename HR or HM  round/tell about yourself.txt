I'm a Senior Frontend Engineer with about 7 years of experience, and most of that time has been at a startup called Exly, where I've had the chance to build things from the ground up and grow into a leadership role.

I joined in early 2020 as a React Native developer. One of my first big contributions was building a group chat feature using a real-time database, and I also optimised the home screen of our mobile app — bringing the Time to Interactive down from 15 seconds to under 5. That kind of performance work became something I got genuinely passionate about.
Then COVID hit. Our company made the decision to pivot from a services model to a full SaaS product, and I was right in the middle of that. I rapidly built a complete frontend framework from scratch to get our MVP to market as quickly as possible. Once we validated usage, I helped grow that into two full-fledged products — a B2B tool for creators and coaches, and a B2C customer-facing website, both built in React and TypeScript.

As our user base scaled, I started noticing architectural pain points. Our customer-facing app had grown into a monolith — homepage, offering pages, and the post-login dashboard all in one repo. I led the decision to eject the dashboard into its own micro-frontend, rebuilt it with a modern stack — Vite, Zustand, Tailwind, Shadcn — and created a shared UI and utilities library so all our repos could stay in sync without duplicating work. That refactor also brought our bundle size down from 30 MB to under 5 MB.
On the mobile side, I built and deployed our React Native app on both iOS and Android using Expo and EAS for the MVP, and later optimised it into a bare React Native CLI app with NativeWind and Gluestack for better performance and native control.
Beyond the technical side, I've been leading and mentoring a frontend team of 7 to 10 developers — setting coding standards, doing architectural reviews, owning frontend deployments, and helping engineers grow. I also enjoy full-stack ownership: one project I'm proud of is our feedback system, which I took from idea to design to backend to deployment. I recently extended it with an LLM integration that evaluates user sentiment and automatically triggers alerts for negative feedback.
Outside of work, I built an LLM-powered code review agent as a side project — it analyses pull requests and gives actionable feedback, something I wanted for my own team.

I'm now looking for a role where I can operate at even greater scale, and Walmart's Marketplace team — with the complexity of merchant tooling, omnichannel experiences, and the sheer number of users — feels like exactly that kind of challenge.
