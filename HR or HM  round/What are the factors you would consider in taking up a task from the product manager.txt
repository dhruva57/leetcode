When taking up a task from a product manager, I try to evaluate it from both a product and technical perspective before committing.

First, I focus on understanding the user and business impact — what problem are we solving, and how critical is it? This helps me prioritize effectively and align with overall goals.

Next, I ensure the requirements are clear. I look for edge cases, user flows, and any ambiguity in the scope. If something isn’t clear, I prefer clarifying upfront rather than making assumptions later.

From a technical standpoint, I assess feasibility and complexity — whether the feature fits well within the existing architecture, if there are any performance considerations, and what kind of state management or data flow it would require.

I also look at dependencies — for example, backend APIs, design readiness, or third-party integrations — and whether those are available or need coordination with other teams.

Another important factor is effort versus timeline. I try to break the task into smaller units, estimate realistically, and, if needed, suggest an MVP approach so we can deliver value faster and iterate.

Finally, I also consider potential risks — like performance bottlenecks, scalability concerns, or integration challenges — and communicate those early so expectations are aligned.

Overall, my goal is to ensure that before starting development, we have clarity on impact, scope, feasibility, and risks, so execution is smooth and predictable.
